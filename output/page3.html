The First Hubble Detection of a Secondary Eclipse from a Rocky Exoplanet: The 5.4 hr Planet TOI-2431 b

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present a Hubble Space Telescope (HST) Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3) G141 phase-curve observation of the ultrashort-period rocky planet TOI-2431 b and detect its secondary eclipse in the near-infrared. Combined with the TESS optical eclipse, these measurements constrain the planet’s dayside brightness temperature, reflected light contribution, albedo, and heat redistribution. We detect the eclipse in the optical and near-infrared wavelengths of TESS and HST/WFC3 G141, with a planet-to-star flux ratio of 37 ± 10 ppm and , respectively. Assuming negligible reflected light, the joint TESS+HST fit yields a brightness temperature and a brightness temperature ratio of consistent with the zero-albedo, no-redistribution limit . The HST phase curve is consistent with zero nightside emission and no measurable hotspot offset, consistent with inefficient day-night heat distribution. Under this thermal-dominated interpretation, TOI-2431 b lies at the zero-albedo, no-redistribution limit and contrasts with previously observed lava worlds. At optical and near-infrared wavelengths, however, thermal emission is degenerate with reflected light. For a geometric albedo of Ag = 0.1, reflected light would contribute approximately 11 ppm in each bandpass, reducing the temperature to . Longer-wavelength observations are required to isolate the thermal component. Planned JWST/MIRI LRS observations are expected to substantially reduce this degeneracy and provide more direct constraints on heat redistribution and possible silicate-atmosphere spectral features. This marks the first secondary eclipse detection of a rocky exoplanet using HST and establishes TOI-2431 b as a benchmark target for studying properties of lava worlds.

Plasma Turbulence and Turbulent Cascade Rates at High Latitudes Using MeerKAT Radio Observations

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →
Radio observations of cosmic sources viewed through the turbulent magnetized plasma surrounding the Sun offer unique insights into the properties of plasma turbulence in the solar corona and solar wind, over a wide range of heliocentric distances and latitudes. Using new observations with the MeerKAT radio telescope (a precursor to the mid-frequency Square Kilometre Array; SKA-Mid), we investigate the amplitude of turbulent density fluctuations in the solar corona at about 10 R⊙, and we then use this information to evaluate the turbulent energy cascade rate and hence the level of turbulence-associated heating. The remote measurements are compared with MHD simulations of the solar corona and inner heliosphere at high heliospheric latitudes and with Parker Solar Probe equatorial measurements. In equatorial regions, the radio source broadening measurements lead to turbulent energy cascade rates ∼5 × 109 erg g−1 s−1 at 10 R⊙, somewhat consistent with global 3D MHD models and in situ observations; however, higher cascade rates are inferred at high heliospheric latitudes. Our analysis reveals significant variations in the turbulent heating rate ( ) and source shapes that are more anisotropic than possible in any smooth spherically symmetric turbulence model. Both of these results highlight the influence of the large-scale structure of the solar corona and/or turbulence intermittency on the heating process closer to the Sun. The results provide new insights into the heating of the solar corona and the acceleration of the fast solar wind, especially in regions at high heliospheric latitudes that are not accessible to in situ measurements.

Variable X-Ray Flux May Explain Variable Helium Escape from the Habitable-zone Exoplanet LHS 1140b

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →
A central goal in exoplanet science is to understand the conditions under which potentially habitable worlds retain atmospheres. This question is of special interest for worlds orbiting M stars, whose habitable-zone planets are more readily observable, but which also emit prolonged high-energy radiation that can erode planetary atmospheres. For one such world, LHS 1140b, a rocky planet orbiting in the habitable zone of an inactive star, time-variable helium absorption has been observed as the planet transited in front of its host star, consistent with atmospheric escape. We present X-ray/ultraviolet flux observations with the XMM-Newton space telescope, which serve to test whether the observed helium absorption variability can be explained by stellar X-ray and extreme-ultraviolet (XUV) flux variability. We detect X-ray flux variability with an amplitude of within the 18 hr observing window. We do not detect variability in the UV or visible flux. The integrated X-ray flux is fully consistent with the X-ray flux measured in 2018, suggesting stable XUV flux over yearslong timescales. We find that the 2018 observation was not sensitive to the level of variability observed in 2026. We explore several models that fit both datasets and conclude that short-term XUV variability and/or small flares are more likely to explain the helium variability than long-term XUV variability. Simultaneous observations of the planet transit in the near-infrared and stellar activity indicators like XUV flux are critical to confirm the link between the observed helium signal variability and the observed X-ray variability.

JWST Reveals a Candidate Supermassive Black Hole Binary at z = 4.3 in the Brightest Submillimeter Galaxy in COSMOS-Web

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and G395M grating spectroscopy for AzTEC-1, a massive submillimeter bright galaxy at z = 4.34 in the COSMOS extragalactic field. The PRISM spectrum reveals strong Hα, a significant Balmer break, and no Hβ detection, indicating a 100–400 Myr old stellar population and high dust attenuation. Baldwin–Phillips–Terlevich (BPT) line ratios indicate the presence of an active galactic nucleus (AGN). Decomposing narrow- and broad-line components, we recover broad, blueshifted Hα with a velocity offset of 1245 km s−1 from the systemic narrow-line velocity and with FWHM ≈ 2500 km s−1. AzTEC-1’s smooth morphology and stellar age is suggestive of a past merger-induced starburst period that would have brought in a second supermassive black hole (SMBH), raising the possibility for a binary SMBH system. In this scenario, we assume that the lower-mass black hole (BH) hosts a broad-line region orbiting a quiescent primary. Evidence for an extended outflow is not found in the 2D spectrum, NIRCam imaging, resolved Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observations of dust continuum, or CO, [C ii]157 μm, and [N ii]205 μm kinematics. AzTEC-1’s high central gas-mass surface density and dynamically unstable gas disk indicate that massive gas clouds external to the candidate binary SMBH’s orbit might have played a role in stalling infall from Myr) to Myr) through dynamical torques, which has been theorized to occur in the nuclei of massive galaxies like AzTEC-1. If the SMBH binary is confirmed, AzTEC-1 would be an excellent laboratory into the astrophysics driving low-frequency gravitational-wave detections.

A Missing Tool for Calculating the Auto/Cross-correlation Functions under Nonuniform Sampling Observations

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Nonuniform sampling presents a long-standing challenge in astrophysical time-domain analysis, invalidating the standard autocorrelation and cross-correlation functions and forcing researchers to adopt ad hoc methods like interpolation or binning, which introduce unquantified biases and lack rigorous error estimation. Here, we introduce a new method for calculating the nonuniform autocorrelation function (NUACF) and nonuniform cross-correlation function (NUCCF) for irregularly sampled time series. Instead of relying on interpolation, it naturally evaluates the correlation function by incorporating time-interval weights and misalignment penalties. Monte Carlo simulations provide confidence bands for significance assessment and a complete error budget for the time delays that accounts for both flux uncertainties and sampling irregularity (essential but generally absent from existing methods). Through extensive simulations, we demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional methods across various conditions, from strictly periodic to complex repeating variability patterns (e.g., intermittent but aperiodic). Its effectiveness is demonstrated via various real astrophysical data sets, revealing repetitive variability in stellar light curves, measuring time delays for multiband disk reverberation in the active galactic nucleus Fairall 9, and providing model-independent validation of time delays for the gravitationally lensed quasar HE 0435-1223. The method provides a rigorous and general solution to the ubiquitous problem of nonuniform sampling, positioning it as a useful tool for large-scale time-domain survey data analysis. The framework is also directly applicable to emerging time-domain phenomena such as fast radio bursts (FRBs), enabling, for example, the study of correlations between persistent radio source luminosity and repeating FRB activity, or among the multiparameter variability curves of FRB emission itself.

Evolution and Geometry of the Heliotail across 14 yr of IBEX-Hi Observations (2009–2022): A Baseline for IMAP-era Heliotail Studies

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
IBEX-Hi observed four heliotail lobes above ∼2 keV: the energetic neutral atom (ENA)-suppressed port (P) and starboard (SB) near the ecliptic and the ENA-enhanced north (N) and south (S) at midlatitudes. They trace how the latitude-dependent solar wind (SW) and solar and interstellar magnetic fields shape the downwind heliosheath (HS). Prior work, using five nonoverlapping periods over 11 yr, found cyclic P–SB motion and equatorward N–S motion in phase with the solar cycle. We extend this analysis through 2022 using 14 smoothed yearly maps and add three methods: an intensity-weighted moment tensor (for lobe shapes), spatial and temporal block uncertainty estimates, and elliptical fits to lobe centers (for motion loci). At ∼4.3 keV, downwind-hemispheric intensity drops ∼2.4× from 2009 to 2016, partially recovering by 2022; all four lobes weaken and recover together, lagging the polar coronal hole area by ∼3 yr. P and SB track the reported cyclic motion; SB shows the largest range (∼52° longitude, ∼36° latitude). N and S move equatorward through 2017 (N ∼59° to ∼27°, S ∼−48° to ∼−24°), partially returning by 2022. The N–S great circle lies near an ecliptic meridian (α ∼ 89 3), P–SB near the ecliptic (β ∼ 23 8), and they are not orthogonal (γ ∼78 1), qualitatively consistent with interstellar field draping around the heliopause. Fitted motion loci constrain models: the P-lobe is least elongated, the S-lobe most elongated, the SB-lobe longest, and the N-lobe least reliable owing to ribbon contribution. Results constrain the distant tailward HS and its evolution with the solar cycle, SW, and charge-exchange cooling.

POSEIDON. III. The Aligned Orbit of the Hot Neptune around the Hot Star WASP-195

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Stellar obliquities provide important clues as to the formation and migration histories of planetary systems, but measurements remain scarce for Neptune-mass planets, especially those orbiting hot stars (above the Kraft break). Here we present observations of the Rossiter–McLaughlin (RM) effect in the hot-star/hot-Neptune system WASP-195 (Teff = 6470 ± 100 K, km s−1) obtained with the Keck Planet Finder and NEID spectrographs. A joint analysis of these observations, archival photometry, and archival radial velocities yields a sky-projected stellar obliquity of λ = −10° ± 7°, consistent with spin–orbit alignment. This makes WASP-195 one of the few hot-star/hot-Neptune systems with a measured obliquity. Archival radial velocities from SOPHIE exclude Jupiter-mass planets within approximately 3 au at 5σ confidence. The aligned and nearly circular orbit is naturally consistent with a history of disk-driven migration, although coplanar high-eccentricity migration or Roche-lobe overflow cannot be ruled out. We also investigate why so few Neptunes around hot stars have measured obliquities. Their scarcity likely reflects a combination of the lower intrinsic occurrence of short-period Neptunes around hot stars and the difficulty of confirming planet candidates in this regime, where rapid stellar rotation broadens spectral lines and hampers conventional radial-velocity confirmation. Rapid rotation also increases the detectability of the RM effect, a feature that could help to widen the planet confirmation bottleneck while expanding the obliquity census of small planets around hot stars.

An Updated Model for ϵ Eridani b and Prospects for Imaging with the Roman Coronagraph

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
ϵ Eridani b, the nearest known Jupiter analog, has its orbit and mass constrained from radial velocity (RV) and absolute astrometry, and its atmosphere from JWST/NIRCam imaging upper limits in A. Sanghi et al. Here we follow up that work with a self-consistent model of ϵ Eridani b that treats all available data within a single Bayesian framework. We extend the RV data with new measurements, update the treatment of the astrometry data with a new model, and include the NIRCam observations with a grid of evolutionary and atmospheric models. We find a mass of 0.91 ± 0.04 MJup and an orbit consistent with previous work. The imaging data helps constrain planet effective temperature, atmospheric metallicity, and surface gravity. The constraints are dependent on model assumptions: for an atmosphere in chemical equilibrium, an otherwise low statistically significant feature in one of the epochs is recovered as the planet at high confidence. When assuming chemical disequilibrium, the posteriors exhibit a bimodal distribution, with one mode consistent with zero flux and the other coinciding with the flux of the tentative feature. Consistent with previous work, the clear atmosphere models are found to be viable at very enhanced metallicity, whereas the cloudy models yield more moderate values. Applying these models to the Roman Coronagraph, we find that the predicted reflected-light fluxes place every cloudy atmosphere our fit allows within the instrument’s expected sensitivity. A nondetection would be very constraining: a final contrast sensitivity of 2 × 10−9 would rule out all cloudy atmospheres under our model assumptions.

Improved Astrometry in a Sparse Star Field and New Positions of Triton and Nereid (2018–2020)

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
High-precision astrometry of Neptunian satellites is fundamental for determining their orbital parameters and can provide clues to the origin and evolution of the Neptunian system. During 2018–2020, Neptune passed through a region at a high galactic latitude (about −58∘), where background Gaia reference stars are relatively sparse. Compounding this sparsity, pronounced geometric distortion is evident even within small CCD fields of view (e.g., ), necessitating modeling with a high-order polynomial. However, applying standard high-order plate models independently to each CCD frame in sparse star fields can lead to overfitting, thereby limiting the astrometric precision of Neptunian satellites. To address this, we propose the PlateOne method, which combines all images taken in a single night to determine a common high-order polynomial. We also investigate the minimum number of stars required for a high-order plate model to avoid overfitting, a crucial yet currently unresolved question. The proposed method has been successfully applied to the astrometry of Triton and Nereid, significantly improving their positional precision. We obtained 451 new CCD positions for Triton, with precisions of 13 mas in right ascension and 18 mas in declination. For Nereid, we obtained eight positions, with a precision of approximately 40–50 mas.

A Search for Dust in the Outflow of the HH 212 Class 0 Protostellar System

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Perhaps the most visually striking phenomenon during star formation is the ejection of material along the direction of the protostar’s rotational axis. Observed in the form of molecular outflows and/or atomic jets, these features carry away mass, energy, and angular momentum from the system. Given the known dust content of protoplanetary disks, and the energetics of outflows, it has been suggested that it should be possible to lift dust from disks via winds, yet limited observational evidence has been presented in the literature. Any detection of dust within protostellar outflows not only puts constraints on the dust content in outflows, but if a sufficient amount of dust can be removed from the system, the mass budget for planet formation could also be affected. We present reimaging of archival, multifrequency dust continuum observations taken with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) toward the HH 212 protostellar system. We detect an extension in the dust emission, and, using multifrequency data in Bands 7 and 9, we estimate the spectral index of the emitting dust and the dust mass in the wind-launching region. We find a small but still significant amount of dust mass coincident with the molecular outflow cavity. This finding is assessed in relation to the hypothesis that the outflow could be capable of transporting dust away from the HH 212 protoplanetary disk.

Exploring the Long-term Temporal Variability in Polarization through Multiepoch Optical Spectropolarimetry. II. A Sample of Symbiotic and Red Giant Stars

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Measuring polarization and its temporal variability in the continuum and across emission features provides a powerful probe of the small-scale circumstellar environments of astrophysical sources, on spatial scales otherwise inaccessible to direct imaging techniques. However, the photon-hungry nature of spectropolarimetry has significantly limited the availability of such datasets in the literature. Here we present the results of a multiepoch spectropolarimetric survey of a sample of symbiotic and red giant stars. These observations were first conducted during the performance verification phase of a recently developed medium-resolution echelle spectropolarimeter, named ProtoPol, mounted on the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) 2.5 m telescope, Mt. Abu, India. Considering the scarcity of such observing samples in the literature, the targets were reobserved over a period exceeding 26 months, from 2024 March to 2026 May, in order to explore the time variability of their polarimetric measurements. Our sample comprises 6 symbiotic stars and 18 red giant stars. Our observations found that, unlike the Raman-scattered λλ 6830, 7088 Å features, the Hα emission in most of the symbiotics did not exhibit any notable polarization signature. However, the continuum polarization of most symbiotics and red giants in the sample showed significant variation between observation epochs. The observations presented here constitute one of the rare multiepoch spectropolarimetric datasets spanning more than 2 yr and should be of considerable interest to the broader astronomical community. This is Paper II of a two-part series of sample studies; corresponding results for Herbig Ae/Be and classical Be stars are presented in Paper I.

Revealing the Extended Stellar Structure of the Binary Cluster NGC 5617–Trumpler 22 with Gaia DR3

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractBinary clusters are important laboratories for studying star formation, the early dynamical evolution of open clusters, and interactions between stellar systems. NGC 5617–Trumpler 22 is a young binary cluster candidate in which the two clusters it contains have similar distances, ages, extinction values, and kinematic properties. The large-scale stellar structure of NGC 5617–Trumpler 22 remains insufficiently explored. As part of the Dali Binary Cluster Project (DL-BCP), this study aims to explore the large-scale stellar structure of the NGC 5617–Trumpler 22 binary cluster system and to search for external substructures associated with it. We used high-precision astrometric and photometric data from Gaia Data Release 3 (Gaia DR3) to analyse a region with a radius of 7.5○ centred on the system. Candidate members were selected by combining proper-motion, parallax, and colour–magnitude diagram (CMD) criteria, resulting in 4 111 candidate member stars. The spatial distribution of these members was further analysed using the density-based clustering algorithm DBSCAN. We identify an extended halo structure around NGC 5617–Trumpler 22, on a scale about six to eight times larger than the core radii of the two clusters. We also detect two previously uncatalogued external substructures at projected distances of about 55.61 and 152.94 pc from the binary cluster centre. These components show similar proper motions and parallaxes, and their CMDs indicate similar ages. Relative tangential motions suggest two possible origins: one may involve stars moving outwards from the central region, whereas the other may be a primordial remnant of the star-formation event that produced both clusters.

Cracking into RACS - I. Measuring the cosmic dipole in the RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 radio surveys with simulation-based inference

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractWe measure the dipole in the count of radio galaxies over the sky as seen by the Australian SKA Pathfinder (ASKAP) in the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey (RACS). Our analysis focuses on the third observational epoch in the ‘low’ passband (943.5 MHz; RACS-low3) and the first epoch in the ‘mid’ passband (1367.5 MHz; RACS-mid1). We find strong evidence for a systematic relation between flux density and the temperature at the time of observation, whereby reported flux densities are suppressed to a greater degree when the ASKAP antennas are warmer. We include this temperature response in our forward model as part of our Simulation-based Inference (SBI) pipeline, generating data realisations that are then used by a neural network to learn the likelihood function. Despite this model, RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 both exhibit a dipole inconsistent with the kinematic dipole of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), adding further evidence for the ‘cosmic dipole tension’. For RACS-low3, the dipole agrees in direction but has an amplitude four times in excess, while the dipole for RACS-mid1 is offset from the CMB and has an amplitude roughly five times in excess. Our findings underscore the importance of interrogating the data for systematic effects and showcase the power of SBI in handling them.

GRB 241030A: A fallback-accreting magnetar description of the early X-ray emission

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractThe bright, variable early X-ray emission of GRB 241030A at z = 1.411 motivates investigating continued central-engine activity before the smoother afterglow emerges. We model a possible underlying magnetar contribution to the unabsorbed 0.3–10 keV XRT flux over 80–430 s, evolving the spin under fallback-disk and electromagnetic torques. A descriptive estimate gives P0 ≃ 4.25 ms, B ≃ 3.04 × 1015 G and source-frame tfb ≃ 143 s, with exploratory temporal-block uncertainty ranges. The effective efficiency reaches its geometry-dependent ceiling of 81.2. At observer time 200 s, this realization has P* ≃ 4.82 ms and magnetospheric, corotation and light-cylinder radii rm ≃ 235 km, rco ≃ 48 km and rLC ≃ 230 km. It is on the ejector branch; light-cylinder entry near 255 s initiates negative propeller torque and steepens the decline. The smooth component declines throughout the fitted interval; additional variable emission contributes to the observed maximum near 180 s. A correlated-residual Bayesian analysis permits a fainter component and broader dynamical histories, with median P0 = 16.8 ms and central 68 per cent interval 2.76–286 ms. However, posterior predictive checks reveal that this statistical description typically underpredicts the observed flux level and variability, limiting its physical interpretation. Fallback-modified magnetar activity remains a possible descriptive scenario, but these analyses do not establish a unique underlying component or robust constraints on the initial spin and disk-interaction history. We also discuss Lense–Thirring precession and alternative mechanisms as possible origins of the early X-ray variability.

Rubin Observatory’s Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC)’s weak lensing 3x2pt pipeline (TXPipe). pop-cosmos: Galaxy size evolution across structural and star-formation classifications in COSMOS-Web

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractGalaxy sizes are correlated with stellar mass and redshift, as characterised by size scaling relations. The inferred forms of these scaling relations are sensitive to how galaxies are classified – either by their star formation activity (e.g. specific star-formation rate, sSFR) or by their morphology markers (e.g. bulge-to-total ratio, Sérsic index). We combine stellar mass and sSFR estimates from pop-cosmos (a generative model trained on COSMOS2020 Spitzer IRAC Ch.  1 < 26) with size and morphology measurements from COSMOS-Web, obtaining 99 369 galaxies. By investigating the size–mass and the size–redshift relations, we show that: (i) the sSFR/morphology splits give quantitatively different slopes, intercepts, and intrinsic scatter behaviour; (ii) intrinsic scatter depends on structural morphology but not on sSFR, which constrains the galaxy–halo connection; (iii) the quiescent and bulge-dominated size–mass relations both show double-power law breaks, but at different pivot masses, indicating that quenching and structural transformation occur on different time-scales; (iv) the morphology-dependent trends are only recoverable from space-based imaging. Further, the quiescent pivot mass M* ~ 1010.7 M⊙ coincides with the mass scale at which the active galactic nuclei (AGN, infrared torus) bolometric luminosity fraction peaks in transitioning galaxies, while the bulge-dominated pivot mass M* ~ 1011.1 M⊙ coincides with the halo mass at which AGN-driven baryonic redistribution peaks, which may trace the interval over which AGN feedback ramps from quenching onset to structural transformation.

First rotational (de-)excitation of HOCS+(X1A′) by collision with He(1S): rate coefficients and potential energy surface

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractIn this work, we constructed the first potential energy surface (PES) for the HOCS+–He complex using the explicitly correlated ab initio CCSD(T)-F12/aug-cc-pVTZ method. An examination of this PES revealed the existence of three potential wells, the deepest of which is 297.25 cm−1, located in the HOCS+ plane, on the hydrogen atom side. We used this PES in dynamical calculations to determine the rate coefficients at which the rotational energy of HOCS+ is exchanged by collisions with He. Rotational transitions involving the lowest 31 energy levels of HOCS+ (up to Erot < 40.2 cm−1) were studied. The quantum close-coupling method (CC) is applied for calculating cross sections at total energies below 50 cm−1. The coupled-states approach (CS) is employed for calculations covering energies up to 500 cm−1. By averaging these cross sections over a Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, we calculated the rate coefficients for temperatures ranging from 2 to 100 K. In the studied temperature range, a slight dominance of rotational transitions with ΔJ = ΔKc = −1 and ΔKa = 0 was observed. The rate coefficients calculated for HOCS+ ion are compared with those of its valence-isoelectronic molecule HOCO+, specifically for collisions involving He. A similarity was observed, especially for low ΔJ. We expect that the new rate coefficients will help to analyze present and future observations of this key molecule for interstellar chemistry.
