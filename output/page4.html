ReveaLLAGN 3: Revealing the Infrared Spectral Energy Distributions of Low Luminosity AGN

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractThe spectral energy distributions of active galactic nuclei (AGN) provide valuable information on the emission mechanisms taking place in their inner regions. Here we use the James Webb Space Telescope to study the near and mid-infrared spectral energy distributions of seven nearby low-luminosity active galaxy nuclei from 1.7 to 24 μm. We cleanly separate the AGN spectrum by performing spatial fits of the two-dimensional images across all wavelengths. We then compare these clean unresolved spectra to aperture-based spectra and show that they effectively remove galaxy contamination. Comparing our infrared measurements to X-ray emission, we find that low-luminosity AGN have enhanced near-infrared emission relative to more luminous AGN, a trend that correlates with radio-loudness, while their mid-infrared-to-X-ray ratios are comparable. A majority of our targets show strong compact silicate emission suggesting the presence of warm dust on parsec scales in these systems. We also quantify the near- and mid-infrared power-law slopes and colors of low-luminosity AGN. In the near-infrared we find AGN of all luminosities have similar spectral slopes and colors, however in the mid-infrared, we find low-luminosity AGN typically have steeper slopes and redder colors than more luminous AGN. Both the strong silicate emission and steep mid-infrared slopes disagree with existing models for low-luminosity AGN spectral energy distributions.

The MeerKAT 1.3 GHz survey of the Large Magellanic Cloud: Point Source Catalogue

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractWe present a point source catalogue of the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) using observations from the MeerKAT radio telescope across a broad frequency band centred at 1295 MHz (L-Band) with 12 sub-bands and a resolution of 8″×8″. We investigate source flux density distributions and spectral indices and compare these with previous similar radio catalogues such as those conducted with the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP) telescope at 888 MHz. This new MeerKAT survey reaches a lower average noise level of 11 μJy beam−1, compared with the previous ASKAP levels of 58 μJy beam−1. This higher sensitivity and resolution allow the detection of a significantly larger number of faint sources. The catalogue includes 339,128 point sources; a substantial increase over the 54,612 sources detected by ASKAP providing critical insights into the population distribution, spectral properties, positional accuracy, and overall improvement in source recovery and survey depth.

High-resolution mapping of pluvial flooding in ungauged agricultural catchments
Matthieu Herpoel, Pierre Baert, Charles Bielders, Gilles Swerts, and Aurore Degré
Hydrol. Earth Syst. Sci., 30, 6661–6699, https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-6661-2026, 2026
Heavy rainfall can cause sudden, damaging floods on farmland, yet most small catchments have no stream gauges, making floods hard to predict. We built a detailed computer model that traces how rain becomes runoff across a well-monitored Belgian farm catchment. It reproduces flood timing reasonably well, but less so for short, intense storms. Applied to extreme rainfall scenarios, it pinpoints flood-prone flow paths, a first step toward better flood planning in unmonitored farmland.

Comparative nitrogen speciation in marine and coastal urban aerosols of the Greater Bay Area and implications for PM2.5-bound nitrogen deposition
Cong Cao, Wei Chen, Xu Yu, Wing Hei Marco Wong, Ningning Sun, Kun Zhang, Weicong Cheng, Jianping Gan, and Jian Zhen Yu
Atmos. Chem. Phys., 26, 15273–15290, https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-26-15273-2026, 2026
Coastal waters receive nitrogen from the air, but its chemical forms are poorly understood. We sampled airborne particles during a cruise in the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macau Greater Bay Area and at an urban site in Hong Kong. Nitrogen chemistry differed between urban and marine air, and carbon-containing nitrogen declined offshore. Fine particles supplied nitrogen to coastal waters, highlighting the need to consider its chemical form when assessing nutrient inputs and potential ecosystem effects.

Seethala Chellappan, David Painemal, Mandana Thieman, Christian Pelayo, William L. Smith Jr., and Lynn M. Russell
Atmos. Chem. Phys., 26, 15201–15221, https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-26-15201-2026, 2026
We analyzed observations from the ARM (Atmospheric Radiation Measurement) EPCAPE (Eastern Pacific Cloud Aerosol Precipitation Experiment) campaign at coastal California, and satellite cloud properties to understand how large-scale weather patterns influence marine low clouds and aerosols. Lake-Mountain synergy in convective clouds and precipitation over the Tibetan Plateau: a large-eddy simulation study
Kai Yang, Jinghua Chen, Tianliang Zhao, Xiangde Xu, Chunsong Lu, Shizuo Fu, Yuehan Luo, Qingjian Yang, Ziqian Liu, Zhikuan Li, Lin Wu, and Yuxiang Jin
Atmos. Chem. Phys., 26, 15179–15199, https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-26-15179-2026, 2026
The Tibetan Plateau has little available moisture and energy, so convection is hard to develop. Large-eddy simulations indicates the lake promotes low-level moistening and lowers the barrier to convective initiation, produces shallow clouds and week rain. Mountain alone force lifting, yet the lack of moisture limits terrain-driven precipitation. Lake-mountain coupling is key: the mountain lifts lake-supplied moisture but also block low-level moisture transport, reshaping precipitation patterns.

Shell hash promotes growth in Pacific littleneck clams (Leukoma staminea) by altering pore water chemistry
Hannah L. Kempf, Alyssa J. Griffin, Gabriel Ng, Tessa M. Hill, Tsim D. Schneider, Sandra J. Carlson, and David A. Gold
Biogeosciences, 23, 7627–7642, https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-23-7627-2026, 2026
We studied whether mixing crushed shell into sediment can help Pacific littleneck clams cope with acidic seawater. In a 90-day experiment, young clams grew better with shell material present, even under acidic conditions. The shell pieces raised sediment water acidity levels in a helpful way, and clam growth looked much like clams in normal seawater. This suggests a simple, practical way to protect valuable shellfish and support healthier coastal ecosystems.

Nat. Hazards Earth Syst. Sci., 26, 5361–5387, https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-26-5361-2026, 2026
Volcanoes often remain in quiescence for long periods before erupting. We reviewed research on how volcanic hazards are communicated during these quieter phases. Floods disrupt roads and bridges, causing delays, higher travel costs and expensive repairs. However, user costs from slower traffic and detours are often ignored.
