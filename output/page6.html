Statistical Characteristics, Occurrence Probabilities, and Extreme-event Recurrence Analysis of Solar Proton Events during 1976–2026

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Using long-term observations of >10 MeV solar proton events (SPEs) during 1976–2026, we investigate their temporal evolution, occurrence probability distributions, and extreme-event recurrence characteristics. The results show that SPE occurrence is strongly correlated with the solar activity cycle, with most events concentrated during the ascending and maximum phases, while Solar Cycle 23 exhibits the highest activity level. The integral occurrence probabilities are well described by power-law functions with exponential cutoffs, whereas the differential distributions approximately follow exponential decay relations, indicating that SPEs exhibit both scale-free behavior and high-energy suppression. The occurrence probabilities of different SPE classes (S1–S4) show asymmetric temporal distributions over the solar cycle, with probability peaks near solar maximum. Extreme value theory (EVT), including the block maxima (BM) and peaks over threshold (POT) approaches, is further applied to estimate the return levels of extreme events. The results suggest that centennial-scale extreme SPEs may reach peak proton fluxes on the order of 105 pfu, while millennial-scale events could exceed several 105 pfu. Among the investigated methods, the BM annual approach shows the best agreement with the long-term statistical fitting results, whereas model uncertainties increase significantly with recurrence time. These findings are important for understanding the statistical behavior of solar energetic particles, assessing extreme space-weather risks, and modeling radiation environments for future deep-space exploration missions.

Enhancing the Interpretability of Radial Velocity Analysis Using Leave-one-out Cross Validation

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Radial velocity (RV) observations are reaching increasingly high precision, enabling the targeting of low-amplitude signals. Assessing results obtained at the limits of our capabilities requires understanding the relationship between data, models and inference. Leave-one-out cross validation (LOO-CV) has become an increasingly popular tool for interpretable model criticism in astronomical data analysis. In this work, we demonstrate how LOO-CV can provide insight into the RV analysis of exoplanetary systems through three case studies. We first investigate the anomalously high mass of HD 119130 b inferred from CARMENES RVs, later revised significantly downward after follow-up observations with other instruments. We show that this high mass was strongly driven by two individual measurements, whose removal significantly reduces the tension between data sets. Next, we use LOO-CV as a model selection tool for the GJ 4276 system. Here, LOO-CV prefers an eccentric single-planet model over a two-planet model, and identifies the orbital phases at which follow-up observations would best discriminate between them. Finally, we extend LOO-CV to models with correlated noise, applying it to the GJ 357 system, whose RVs are modeled with a Gaussian process. We find no predictive support for a fourth planetary signal that is moderately favored by the Bayesian evidence, strengthening the interpretation of a three-planet architecture previously reported in the literature. We provide practical guidance for implementing LOO-CV in RV analysis, together with an online tutorial. Such methods will become ever more important as future RV surveys push the limits of planet detection and characterization.

Investigating Stellar Limb-darkening Laws Using the Navy Precision Optical Interferometer Data Archive

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
The gradual decrease in intensity from a star’s center to its edge is known as limb darkening, and it acts as a critical diagnostic for understanding stellar atmospheres. This paper takes a collection of previously published stars from the Navy Precision Optical Interferometer data archive to evaluate the performance of several limb darkening laws (linear, quadratic, power-4, pure power) and multiple models (ATLAS, Stagger, Phoenix, Phoenix NewEra). We present a catalog with our results, and compare the minimum and maximum limb-darkened angular diameters from the fits.

PEACC—Precision Emitter for 21 cm Array Coherent Calibration

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Foreground mitigation remains a central challenge for 21 cm intensity mapping experiments, which require precise, wideband calibration of telescope beams and gains. We present the Precision Emitter for 21 cm Array Coherent Calibration (PEACC), a digitally synthesized calibration source that generates Gaussian noise across a 1.2 GHz bandwidth, time-synchronized to a 1 pulse-per-second output from a GPS-disciplined oscillator, and optimized for aerial deployment. PEACC uses a dual-source architecture with one unit mounted on an aerial platform and a second reference unit connected directly to the radio data acquisition system; this configuration enables improved sensitivity in the low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime and direct phase measurement. The system further supports configurable band selection, allowing adaptation to various 21 cm intensity mapping telescopes. We validated PEACC through anechoic chamber measurements and by integrating the source on a drone flown over a local radio dish testbed. In both settings, the correlated channel substantially outperformed the autocorrelation channel across all signal-to-noise regimes of interest, confirming the key advantage of the dual-source architecture. To our knowledge, this is the first published demonstration of a free-space coherent calibration signal synchronized only by clocks, the first deployment of such a source on a drone, and the first published beam measurements made with such a source. Given the growing interest in drone-based calibration for 21 cm arrays, this work establishes the feasibility of high-fidelity digital calibration for next-generation 21 cm instruments and provides a practical path towards improved foreground control and beam calibration in future arrays.

Deep Hα Imaging Survey of IC 348 with the Hubble Space Telescope. I. Accretion Properties of Stellar and Substellar Objects

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Accretion governs the growth of young stars and the early evolution of their circumstellar disks, yet population-level measurements of accretion are often hampered by heterogeneous diagnostics and by samples preferentially selected toward disk-bearing or accreting objects. This can impact the mass accretion rate–stellar mass ( –M⋆) relation, particularly at substellar masses. We present a uniform analysis of accretion in the ∼2 Myr old star-forming cluster IC 348 based on deep Hubble Space Telescope (HST) F656N imaging. Using Hα excess as a single, homogeneous accretion tracer, we derive accretion rates and robust upper limits for 200 cluster members spanning the stellar-to-substellar mass regime (3 M⊙ to 4 MJup). Accretion is detected in 37% ± 3% of the sample, with fractions of 34% ± 4% among stellar members and 46% ± 6% among substellar objects. For accretors alone, the inferred –M⋆ relation is consistent with those measured in similarly aged regions such as Lupus. In contrast, including weak accretors and nondetections increases the scatter and flattens the slope while lowering the intercept, demonstrating the strong influence of the low-accretion tail on population-level accretion relations. For free-floating planetary-mass objects in IC 348, extrapolating the accretors-only fit overpredicts by approximately an order of magnitude compared to the fit that includes upper limits, which instead implies mass accretion rates of <10−12M⊙ yr−1. These results show that sample selection—specifically whether the sample is restricted to disk-bearing or accreting targets or instead is drawn from a complete membership census—is a dominant factor shaping population-level accretion relations across a wide dynamic range in stellar mass.

SACRA-K: A Performance-portable Numerical Relativity Code with Kokkos

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present the SimulAtor for Compact objects in Relativistic Astrophysics with Kokkos (SACRA-K), a performance-portable numerical relativity (NR) code ported from the Fortran code SACRA-MPI to C++ with the Kokkos library. SACRA-K retains the physics and numerical methods of SACRA-MPI, namely, a Baumgarte–Shapiro–Shibata–Nakamura (BSSN) spacetime evolution with Z4c constraint propagation and a box-in-box Berger–Oliger adaptive mesh refinement, together with the high-resolution shock-capturing scheme for the general relativistic hydrodynamics from NANASI, while gaining cross-vendor portability. We validate the port against SACRA-MPI across binary black hole, black hole neutron star, and binary neutron star (BNS) configurations. Across these tests, the waveform discrepancies are well below both the scatter among independent NR codes and the resolution dependent variation within a given code, and remain at or below the distinguishability threshold of current gravitational-wave (GW) detectors; the π symmetry is preserved exactly at the bitwise level; and the GW phase of the BNS merger exhibits second-order convergence. In the smallest test configuration, SACRA-K runs about an order of magnitude faster on the NVIDIA A100 GPU cluster or the AMD MI300A APU cluster than the Fortran SACRA-MPI on the CPU cluster, and we measure its scaling up to 256 accelerator devices.

FASHI DR2: A Catalog of 132 Low-redshift H I 21 cm Absorption Systems

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present an untargeted survey of 21 cm H i absorption systems based on the second data release of the FAST All Sky H I survey (FASHI DR2), covering approximately 19,500 deg2 at z ≲ 0.09. A total of 132 H i absorbers are identified, including approximately 60 new discoveries, forming one of the largest homogeneous samples of low-redshift H i absorbers assembled to date. The sample extends to continuum flux densities as low as 2.6 mJy, substantially below the limits of previous flux-limited surveys. The absorber population is dominated by narrow systems (W50 < 100 km s−1), while broad absorbers (W50 > 200 km s−1) account for 13.6% of the sample. Most absorbers are optically thin, with a median optical depth of τHI ≈ 0.14. The velocity-offset distribution is broadly symmetric about the systemic velocities of the host galaxies. The associated absorbers are preferentially found in massive, actively star-forming galaxies. We find tentative evidence for a weak anticorrelation between H i column density and stellar mass, although the relation exhibits substantial scatter. These results provide the first statistical characterization of the low-redshift H i absorber population based on the FASHI DR2 sample and establish a valuable benchmark for future H i absorption surveys with next-generation radio facilities.

Stellar Parameters and Magnetic Activity of Eruptive Variable Stars Based on TESS Light Curves and the LAMOST Spectral Survey

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Eruptive variable stars are an important type of variable stars. We investigate the chromospheric activity and white-light flare properties of International Variable Star Index (VSX)-selected eruptive stars by combining LAMOST DR10 low-resolution spectroscopy with TESS 2 minute cadence flare measurements. The LAMOST spectroscopic parent sample contains 3099 unique VSX-selected eruptive-star targets with 9132 low-resolution spectra, and the quality-controlled Hα subset contains 2913 unique targets and 8259 spectra. The adopted TESS flare catalog contains 6665 flare events from 453 TESS Input Catalog (TIC) targets, among which 6621 flare events from 450 TIC targets have successful VSX matches. The final statistical sample is treated as a magnetic-activity-dominated eruptive-star population, with RS CVn systems analyzed separately from other main-sequence eruptive stars. The Hα equivalent widths used in this work are independently measured from LAMOST spectra, and the excess Hα emission and normalized activity index are used to compare chromospheric activity across spectral type. The Hα activity indicators show a clear temperature dependence, with cooler stars generally displaying stronger and more broadly distributed chromospheric emission, whereas earlier-type bins require cautious interpretation. Multiepoch LAMOST spectra reveal Hα variability in both RS CVn systems and other main-sequence eruptive stars, although the variability indicators show substantial scatter. The TESS flare population is dominated by short-duration and low-to-moderate-amplitude events, with higher-energy flares forming a sparser extension.

The Galactic Black Hole Mass Distribution

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
The population of extragalactic black holes has been significantly expanded and better understood since the first detection of gravitational waves. Over the past decade, approximately 290 events have been confirmed to involve at least one black hole. In contrast, the Galactic black hole population has grown at a much slower pace, with only 42 systems possessing mass estimates, while the vast majority remain as candidates. In this work, we present the first update of a Bayesian analysis of the mass distribution of Galactic black holes since 2011, previously based on a sample of 15 low-mass X-ray binaries (LMXBs) and five high-mass X-ray binaries (HMXBs). Our update includes 28 LMXBs, six HMXBs, seven detached binaries, and one isolated black hole, also present in the living catalog CatNoir, which we introduce. We apply a Bayesian framework using five parametric models—Gaussian, two-Gaussian, lognormal, power-law, and decaying exponential—and compare their performance in fitting the observed data. Furthermore, we inspect the effect of removing model-dependent systems from the sample. Our results suggest that the power law better describes the Combined sample, while the Gaussian is preferred for the LMXB-only sample, closely followed by the two-Gaussian and lognormal. We derive the 1% quantile of the preferred model’s posterior predictive distribution to access information about the lower-mass gap. For the Combined and LMXB samples, we find M1% > 4.06 M⊙ and M1% > 2.56 M⊙ (at 90% credible intervals), respectively. The LMXB result allows for the existence of a sparsely populated gap, instead of a completely empty one.

Improved Hot Line Lists for Minor CN Isotopologues in the X 2Σ+, A 2Π, and B 2Σ+ Systems Using Diagonal Born–Oppenheimer Corrections

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present improved hot line lists for optical transitions between the mutually perturbed B2Σ+, A2Π, and X2Σ+ states of the minor CN isotopologues 13C14N, 12C15N, and 13C15N. Accurate line positions and intensities for these isotopologues are required for high-resolution analyses of stellar CN red-system spectra and cometary CN violet-band fluorescence, where minor-isotopologue features are used to infer carbon and nitrogen isotopic ratios. The rovibronic energies and transition probabilities were recalculated by explicitly incorporating mass-dependent diagonal Born–Oppenheimer corrections (DBOCs) into the semiempirical potential energy curves recently derived for the B, A, and X states for the main 12C14N isotopologue. For the X and A states, the radial DBOC functions were initially estimated during electronic structure calculations accomplished at the CCSDT/cc-pVDZ level of theory. The inclusion of the ab initio DBOCs significantly improves the accuracy of the A−X and B−X positions by reducing the root-mean-square error (RMSE) in the description of the experimental A−X and B−X lines of the 13C14N [12C15N] isotopologues from 0.24 [0.15] and 0.16 [0.05] cm−1 to 0.04 [0.01] and 0.08 [0.05] cm−1, respectively. A subsequent simultaneous adjustment of the DBOC functions for all three B, A, and X states to the experimental 13C14N lines further decreased the RMSE of the predicted wavenumbers for the minor 13C14N and 12C15N isotopologues to the spectroscopic level of accuracy. The new KTPSYT-iso line lists are made available via www.exomol.com.
