Mauricio Herrera-Marín, Francisca Kleisinger, Diego Rivera, Andrés Wilson, and Alex Godoy-Faundez
Hydrol. Earth Syst. Sci., 30, 6531–6547, https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-6531-2026, 2026
Chile’s changing rainfall makes water planning more difficult. Using decades of rainfall observations and climate records, we identified five recurring weather patterns and simulated rainfall across the country. Deciphering the Green Monster in Cassiopeia A: Puncturing and sculpting a heterogeneous circumstellar shell

by S. Orlando, H.-T. Janka, D. Milisavljevic, I. De Looze, T. Temim, R. Fesen, B.-C. Koo, M. Miceli, F. Bocchino 2026, A&A, 714, A328

In an extensive set of simulations of a supernova and its environment, Orlando and collaborators have followed the evolution of the Cas A event from explosion to the present day and beyond. In this latest result, they examine the effect of the shock striking out into a preexisting circumstellar shell. The resulting fine structure induced by the individual knots of the ejecta that precede the main forward shock as it punches through this medium is compared with recent JWST observations of "hole-ring" structures, revealing a very heterogeneous circumstellar medium. This work has fundamental implications for many transients, developing a new way of understanding how fragmented shocks affect observables in cases as diverse as classical novae, wind-blown Wolf-Rayet bubbles, and fliers in H II regions and planetary nebulae.

JWST detection of methane emission from a young brown dwarf

by K. L. Luhman, C. Alves de Oliveira, J. K. Faherty, B. C. Smith 2026, A&A, 714, L15

When spectroscopically observing young star-forming regions, researchers frequently detect emission lines from stellar members such as Herbig Ae/Be and T Tauri stars. These signatures —most notably atomic lines of hydrogen, helium, and calcium— stem from circumstellar disc accretion, chromospheric activity, or a combination of both. This same phenomenon is observed when scaling down in mass to investigate million-year-old brown dwarfs (objects below the hydrogen-burning mass limit), and it even persists below the deuterium-burning limit (~13 MJup). However, the planetary-mass regime introduces unique anomalies. In a recent study, Luhman et al. used the James Webb Space Telescope to detect robust methane emission from the upper atmosphere of LRL 11001, a ~6-MJup object in the ~5 Myr-old IC 348 star-forming region. This marks the first discovery of methane emission in a brown dwarf of any mass. The authors suggest its origin may be due to either an accretion shock or external radiation from neighboring cluster members.

Historical climate extremes in Europe and the connection between spring precipitation and summer heat
Laura Lipfert, Ralf Hand, Angela-Maria Burgdorf, Christian Pfister, Heinz Wanner, and Stefan Brönnimann
Clim. Past, 22, 1863–1879, https://doi.org/10.5194/cp-22-1863-2026, 2026
We analyse the link between dry springs and hot summers in Europe over the past 600 years. Starting with a study of the heat summer of 1473, which followed dry spring conditions in Southeastern Europe, we use the ModE-RA data set family to statistically analyse the link. Hot summers are preceded by increased blocking over north-central Europe and a positive East Atlantic pattern in spring.

A first GLIMPSE into star-cluster populations across cosmic time

by A. Claeyssens, A. Adamo, V. Kokorev,et al. 2026, A&A, 714, A148

Star clusters represent the smallest structures in galaxies, sharing a common origin and tracing the star formation history of their hosts. Due to their faint luminosities and compact sizes, detailed observations have historically been limited to nearby systems. In this recently accepted paper, strongly lensed regions (located behind massive galaxy clusters) were targeted with JWST/NIRCam to identify the most distant star clusters. The team studied a sample of 218 star clusters at unprecedented redshifts, with 75% of the sample located between z=0.5 and z=4. This work is part of the GLIMPSE collaboration, which targets strongly lensed regions to explore distant galaxies. All stellar clusters are highly magnified (mu>10), and the observations mostly indicate young ages, corresponding to a formation epoch at cosmic noon (1<4), with some clusters reaching stellar ages of about 1 Gyr. The sample exhibits a wide diversity in mass, size, and stellar surface density. The sample size enables the determination of the cluster mass function at z>1, revealing a power law with a slope around -2. 5. Galactic structure, stellar clusters and populations

Expanding stellar associations as Galactic accelerometers

by Till Sawala, Núria Miret Roig 2026, A&A, 713, A261

The mass distribution of the Milky Way remains one of the largest variables that bears relevance to our understanding of galactic structure and formation. This study introduces a novel method to characterize and precisely quantify the Galactic gravitational potential by using the time evolution of stellar associations. These are ensembles of coeval and spatially coherent, albeit expanding, stars with on the order of tens of members, typically, and ages of a few tens of million years. By using statistical metrics (namely a matrix description of the the positional covariances) to describe the spatial interdistance of a given star group as test cases over time, the authors succeeded at placing constraints on the shape and overall mass of the underlying potential. This is particularly valuable in light of the upcoming fourth data release (DR4) of Gaia at the end of this year: at its superior astrometric and velocity precision, the authors demonstrate that a single association alone would be able to recover the Galaxy’s underlying halo mass to a precision at the 0.6x1012 Mo level, although parameter degeneracies are inescapable.

The JWST weather report: Unravelling the atmospheric variability of isolated worlds using principal component analysis

by Merle A. Schrader, Johanna M. Vos, Evert Nasedkin, Jennifer Kestell, et al. 2026, A&A, 713, A192

SIMP J013656.5+093347.3 (SIMP 0136) is a young, T2.5-type brown dwarf at the planetary-mass boundary, located at a distance of only 6 pc. In addition, it is one of the most photometrically variable field brown dwarfs known, if not the most variable. It was observed with JWST/NIRSpec PRISM over a rotation period of just 2.4 h. The authors analyzed the principal components of the low-resolution 1--5 mum spectroscopic time series and found that most of the brown dwarf's variability can be explained by changes in just two dominant components: temperature and cloud vertical structure. This allowed them to describe the brown dwarf's spectral variability as a linear combination of three distinct atmospheric states.

A large population of overmassive black hole quasars at z=0.3-0.8 revealed by eROSITA

by Johannes Buchner, Isabelle Gauger, Qiaoya Wu, Hattie Starck, Catarina Aydar, et al. 2026, A&A, 713, A11

In the standard framework of galaxy formation and evolution, the two main components -- dark matter and baryonic (gas and stellar) mass -- are complemented by the presence of a central (super)massive black hole (BH). This BH is expected to shape galaxy evolution, sometimes substantially, by regulating the transformation of gas into stars. The mass fraction contributed by the BH is typically about one percent of the host galaxy’s stellar mass. However, recent observations have identified exceptions where the BH mass is much larger. Such overmassive systems (with BH-to-stellar mass ratios exceeding 5\%) have been observed in low-redshift dwarf galaxies and also at very high redshifts. This new A&A publication is based on a large-scale eROSITA X-ray survey, complemented by NUV/optical/IR photometric and spectroscopic data. The authors studied a sample of quasars that are undergoing rapid mass growth, uncovering a significant population (10–20 per cubic Gpc) of galaxies with overmassive central BHs at intermediate redshifts (between z=0.3 and 0.8). The authors suggest a rapid, exponential growth phase of the central BH decoupled from host galaxy development. This growth mode does not require super-Eddington rates and is not included in current galaxy evolution models. In the context of high redshift overmassive BHs, the newly discovered galaxies may suggest an alternative growth channel at cosmic dawn.

3. Cosmology (including clusters of galaxies)

Hidden Vela supercluster revealed by the first hybrid redshift and peculiar velocity reconstruction

by A.M. Hollinger, H.M. Courtois, R.C. Kraan-Korteweg, and al. 2026, A&A, 713, A4

The large-scale distribution of the Universe is characterized by an intricate web of galaxies with low and high density regions. The structure is remarkably variable over all scales, from groups of a few galaxies to the largest possible distributions. Superclusters represent the highest densities in this cosmic web over vast scales, up to 0.1 Gpc. The study of these structures is hampered by a number of pitfalls, most notably foreground dust, which selectively obscures regions and hinders a clean selection of galaxies over the apparent scales expected in these structures. In a study accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics, researchers constructed a catalog of about eight thousand new galaxy redshifts in the Zone of Avoidance (ZoA) near the Galactic plane. The methodology follows a hybrid approach that reconstructs the density contrast and peculiar velocity fields. The team investigated nine superclusters with a special emphasis on the effect of these new ZoA redshifts. The results reveal that the mass and density contrast of the Vela supercluster are considerably larger than previously thought, and a double-core internal structure is claimed. By contrast, the remaining superclusters showed relatively minor structural changes.

2. Astrophysical processes

The exponential growth of infinitesimal perturbations in the long-term evolution of simulated galaxies

by T. Asano, S. Portegies Zwart 2026, A&A, 712, A233

It is well known that self-gravitating systems of N particles are chaotic. In this work, Asano and Portegies Zwart investigated the consequence of this chaos on galaxy stability and morphology. They carried out calculations using a softened gravitational N-body tree-code, with varying numbers of particles, N, up to 40 million. They ran several simulations, each with different copies of the initial conditions with small perturbations, and determined the Lyapunov timescale, after which the chaos settled. This timescale varies as the square root of N but is independent of the time step. Surprisingly, the timescale for bar instability (1.8 Gyr) is insensitive to these infinitesimal perturbations. Only the bar strength and its further evolution sensitively depend on such perturbations. All the bars eventually buckle, but this occurs over a timescale ranging from approximately 2.8 Gyr to 4.2 Gyr. Smoothing the gravitational force over a larger softening length tends to suppress chaos. Softening prevents the system from behaving as chaotically as a granular system, since the dominant driver of chaos occurs on a scale shorter than the softening length. For a purely stellar galaxy disk of Milky Way size without softening, the chaos timescale is estimated to be shorter than 0.1 Myr.
