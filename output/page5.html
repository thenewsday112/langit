Little Red Dots are Direct-collapse Black Hole–forming Galaxies

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
“Little red dots” (LRDs) at 4 < z < 8 are one of the most challenging discoveries by JWST to date because their distinctive V-shaped spectra and compact morphologies defy conventional astrophysical interpretation. Previous attempts to explain LRDs as compact stellar systems, heavily cocooned black holes (BHs) with differential flows, supermassive stars, or more exotic objects like “black hole stars” cannot either show how they formed, explain the origin of the dense shells needed for the absorption features in their spectra, or account for their observed abundances or inferred lifetimes. Here we show that LRDs are simply direct-collapse BH galaxies in which the BH is still shrouded by the massive disk that created it. Our cosmological simulations yield spectra that are good matches to those of LRDs because high densities at the center of the disk trap X-rays from the BH and produce the observed Balmer absorption features while allowing UV, optical, and reprocessed IR flux to partly escape. The host galaxy forms a dense 108M⊙ cluster of stars with a radius of 150 pc next to the BH, consistent with observations of LRDs. Our models reproduce a wide variety of LRD spectra, from typical objects like RUBIES-EGS-42046 at z = 5.28 to those with the strongest Balmer breaks such as MoM-BH*−1 at z = 7.76 and those at the highest redshifts like CAPERS-LRD-z9 at z = 9.29.

A Recurrent Infrared Modulation in the Warm Postimpact Debris Disk NGC 2547 ID8

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Extreme debris disks are signposts of violent rocky collisions, but it remains unclear whether their mid-infrared variability traces transient dust clouds or longer-lived postimpact reservoirs. We analyze a 19 yr mid-infrared record of the young solar analog NGC 2547 ID8, combining four spectra from 2007 to 2024 with 3–5 μm monitoring through 2026. The optically thin mineral surface remains compositionally stable: broad fits spanning multiple grain sizes give 550–577 K, and a common-basis decomposition with Tsurf fixed at 560 K is dominated by 1 μm olivine/Mg-olivine and 0.1 μm forsterite. The W2-equivalent 4.5 μm excess, traced across six recurrent high states, is instead driven mainly by changes in the hot-base continuum component and defines a 956.8 days infrared recurrence timescale with cycle-to-cycle variations in timing and amplitude. This combination supports the interpretation of ID8 as an early postimpact stage in which material on eccentric orbits repeatedly interacts with a warm rocky reservoir, preserving the impact’s mineral fingerprint while the interaction geometry evolves. Material on eccentric orbits can persist for decades, and possibly longer, while interacting with debris disks. Longer-baseline, higher-cadence infrared monitoring of ID8 will further constrain the stability of its recurrence timescale and the detailed evolution of its infrared variability.

Extreme PeV Accelerator Associated with GRS 1915+105

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Microquasars, binary systems featuring relativistic jets, have emerged as sources for particle acceleration beyond PeV energies. We present a study of the broadband γ-ray emission from one of the most prominent Galactic microquasars, GRS 1915+105, based on data accumulated by LHAASO and Fermi Large Area Telescope over 4 and 17 yr, respectively. A joint analysis of LHAASO-WCDA and LHAASO-KM2A data reveals extended γ-ray emission whose centroid appears significantly shifted, by ∼0 13, from the binary system and its jets. The spectral energy distribution is well described by a curved spectrum with progressive steepening that can be described by a log-parabola function with no evidence for a sharp cutoff, consistent with parent particles reaching multi-PeV energies and an extreme acceleration efficiency approaching the limit set by the available potential drop across the source. Several features, most notably the shift of the emission and single power-law spectrum down to GeV band, favor radiation by cosmic rays accelerated in the source interacting with the dense ambient medium. Our spectral modeling implies that at least a few percent of the jet mechanical power is transferred to protons, whose maximum energy reaches beyond 5 PeV. These results strengthen the case for microquasars as exceptionally efficient accelerators in our Galaxy.

ALMA Observations of DEM L241/LMC P3 in the Large Magellanic Cloud: Evidence for the Formation of Cool Molecular Jets Driven by a Microquasar

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present ALMA observations of DEML 241/LMC P3, the most luminous γ-ray binary consisting of a compact object and an O star, in CO emission. We have found a one-sided jet-like CO feature of 8 pc length and 1 pc width, which accompanies another weaker CO jet candidate with slightly different orientation. The one-sided CO jet exhibits striking alignment with LMC P3, suggesting that the jet was driven by LMC P3. We have determined the kinetic temperature of the CO jet to be significantly high at 33−60 K as compared with ∼15 K in the nearby nonjet CO cloud, where no radiative heat source is found. We interpret that the high temperatures are due to shock heating of a microquasar jet driven by the γ-ray binary, where the compact object has an accretion disk fed by the O star winds. The CO jet matches existing predictions from magnetohydrodynamical simulations, which show that CO jet can form from the interaction of the microquasar jet and an ambient interstellar medium (ISM) cloud. These results provide strong evidence that CO jets are a signature sculpted by microquasar jets, lending support for mass accretion in LMC P3 as the γ-ray origin. The results suggest a second case of CO jets potentially driven by a microquasar along with the CO jets in the microquasar candidate HESS J1023-575 recently identified in the Milky Way. Furthermore, our results suggest the use of submillimeter observations for identifying microquasars, opening a new possible window for their discovery and study.

Identification of Magnetic Reconnection in Plasma Turbulence Using a Dual-branch Lightweight Hybrid Convolutional Neural Network

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Magnetic reconnection is a fundamental plasma process that plays a crucial role in energy dissipation in plasma turbulence, in which magnetic energy is rapidly converted into particle energy. However, the identification of reconnection within turbulence remains a significant challenge. To automatically detect magnetic reconnection in plasma turbulence, we propose a novel and efficient classifier called a dual-branch lightweight hybrid convolutional neural network (DHCNN), which, for the first time, achieves automatic localization of reconnection sites by effectively leveraging negative-valued information in physical variables. We adopt a temporal partitioning scheme together with diverse data augmentation strategies to enhance the model’s generalization ability. Our results show that the DHCNN accurately identifies reconnection sites, achieving a Matthews correlation coefficient (M) of 0.67, a true skill statistic (T) of 0.67, a recall (R) of 0.83, a specificity (S) of 0.84, and an accuracy (A) of 0.84. Finally, by analyzing the impact of different physical variables, we find that the total current density (|J|), in-plane components of the magnetic field (Bx, Bz), and energy conversion term (De) are the primary drivers of the model’s performance.

Discovery and Characterization of the TOI-4468 Planetary System: A Transiting Hot Jupiter with a Lone Nearby Outer Companion

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We report the discovery of two planets, a hot Jupiter (HJ) and a nearby outer sub-Neptune, orbiting the star TOI-4468. This system is unique among the current exoplanet census in that it features a close outer companion to a HJ without an accompanying inner companion. By jointly fitting radial velocity (RV) measurements taken with the NEID spectrograph and transit photometry from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) and several ground-based observatories, we constrain the orbital periods, masses, and radii of these two planets. We confirm the planetary nature of the HJ TOI-4468 b (R = 1.01RJ, m = 0.54MJ, P = 2.77 days). We also validate the outer planet TOI-4468 c (R = 0.28RJ, P = 7.01 days) statistically, incorporating constraints from ground-based observations. We also identify, but cannot confirm, an additional RV signal that may be due to an outer giant in this system with an orbital period of 624 days. From the observed geometry of this system, we argue that it must never have encountered an early secular resonance that is thought to excite the mutual inclination of other HJ/outer companion systems. We discuss the possibility of an undetected inner companion, as well as potential implications for HJ formation.

Pulsar glitches due to triggered vortex traps

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractPulsar glitches are thought to originate when angular momentum is transferred to the crust of the neutron star from the superfluid enclosed within, mediated by vortex avalanches. This idea has been qualitatively validated in the existing literature by simulating a star with a small number (∼103) of superfluid vortices, subject to deceleration and uniform pinning. Here, we employ the Barnes-Hut approximation to simulate up to 105 vortices in a reasonable time. Using the new setup, we probe glitches that originate in the presence of inhomogeneous pinning. The inner crust of a neutron star is expected to crack as the star spins down, relieving stresses and resulting in rearrangements within the crustal lattice. These regions become centres for pinning with large pinning energies. Many such vortex traps are expected to exist in mature pulsars like Vela. We simulate one such star and find that the rise of the glitch is now staggered, a clear signature of the trap network. In young to middle-aged stars like PSR J0537-6910, we expect quakes which result in new traps and also unpin the vortices in existing ones. We observe that such a configuration involving the simultaneous release of several traps could introduce a bimodality in the glitch-size distribution, a feature that has previously been reported for PSR J0537-6910. Our simulations also indicate that macroscopic inhomogeneities in the distribution of vortices could spontaneously develop in a star with uniform pinning sites.

Atmospheric Turbulence Profiling in the Lenghu Site Area Using MERRA-2 Data

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
AbstractThe Lenghu site, in the Saishiteng Mountains of the northern Tibetan Plateau, has been identified as an excellent astronomical site from preliminary statistics of clear nights, sky background brightness, precipitable water vapour, total seeing and weather. Since 2022, several projects with apertures larger than 4 m have started construction or are being planned. Given the use of adaptive optics (AO) on these projects, in-depth measurements and analysis of the atmospheric turbulence above the site are required: the distribution and variation of the turbulence profiles provide a large-scale, climatological basis for site planning and for comparing candidate locations, complementing rather than replacing the on-site measurements needed for instrument design and operations. Predicting the atmospheric profiles of the site and its surroundings from satellite data, combined with on-site measurements, can help to optimise the site planning. This paper uses MERRA-2 reanalysis data to study the atmospheric profiles of the site and its environment, revealing the seasonal and long-term variations of the observing conditions. The reanalysis results are cross-checked against on-site ground-level instruments serving the same purpose. MERRA-2 overestimates the median free-atmosphere seeing by +0.22″ (+50%) while keeping the night-to-night correlation weak (r ≈ 0.2), and underestimates the median total seeing by −0.24″ (−27%). It captures only ∼22% of the ground-layer turbulence and little of the observed seasonal amplitude of the free atmosphere. MERRA-2 is therefore suited to relative comparisons of spatial variability between locations and to climatological characterisation, not to quantitative AO applications or night-by-night assessment of profiles.

Mobility of dry granular flows over contrasting erodible bed materials in laboratory experiments
Katharina Wetterauer, Sebastian Müller, Michael Krautblatter, Shiva P. Pudasaini, and Ivo Baselt
Nat. Hazards Earth Syst. Sci., 26, 5315–5338, https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-26-5315-2026, 2026
Contrasting mobility of dry granular flows over erodible beds remains poorly understood. We experimentally investigate erosion, flow evolution, and deposition across bed materials with contrasting solid particle densities. Low-density beds show stronger erosion and longer runout, whereas equal- and high-density beds show similar, shallower erosion and shorter runout. The results show that mobility reflects interacting material properties rather than density contrasts alone.

An Extremely Bright Slow-rising Afterglow from an Off-axis Jet in GRB 260310A

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
We present a multiwavelength study of GRB 260310A, a nearby long-duration gamma-ray burst (GRB) at z ≃ 0.153 associated with a broad-lined Type Ic supernova. Despite its modest prompt gamma-ray output, Eγ,iso ≃ 3.5 × 1050 erg, GRB 260310A exhibits one of the brightest afterglows ever observed in the X-ray, optical, and radio bands. Its apparent brightness is not its only remarkable feature. The optical afterglow displays a delayed onset, characterized by a slow rising phase, with slope α ≈ −1, and a late peak at ∼0.1 day. We argue that the combination of weak prompt emission, hard peak energy, and late afterglow onset is naturally explained by a GRB jet viewed off-axis. The radio spectral energy distributions are consistent with synchrotron radiation and indicate the presence of both reverse- shock (RS) and forward-shock (FS) components, thus providing a first test of RS models in an off-axis geometry. The X-ray afterglow displays a prominent rebrightening, monitored for up to ∼68 days with no evidence of spectral evolution. A low level of linear polarization, Π ≈ 1.7%, is measured at 15 GHz at T0 + 55 days and suggests that, at these late times, the FS is the dominant emission component from radio to X-rays. This late-time rebrightening represents a critical test for the two-component jet model. If interpreted as the emergence of a narrow jet core viewed further off-axis, it would imply extreme luminosities and energetics for an on-axis observer.

Model-driven Stellar Spectroscopy with the Total Differential Spectral Model (TDSM): Framework and Its Application to 11 Million LAMOST Low-resolution Spectra

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Deriving stellar astrophysical parameters from large-scale spectroscopic surveys requires accurate modeling of spectra across a wide wavelength coverage and a broad range of stellar parameter space, which remains a great challenge. In this work, we present the Total Differential Spectral Model (TDSM), a physically motivated neural network architecture that self-consistently emulates both the flux and its first-order derivatives of the ab initio stellar spectra. The method enables accurate and precise emulation of Kurucz synthetic spectra with severely blended features at R ≃ 2000 in a high-dimensional parameter space, achieving a typical pixel-wise flux precision of 0.05% in the wavelength range 3700 < λ < 9000 Å over the parameter ranges of 3000 < Teff < 45,000 K, , and −4.0 < [Fe/H] < 0.5. The model is applied to LAMOST DR10 to derive stellar parameters for 11 million spectra spanning the full model parameter range. We conduct detailed refinement and calibration efforts to mitigate systematic errors, which are revealed to be significant and complex due to imperfections in the ab initio model atmosphere. The resultant catalog provides eight stellar labels—Teff, , vmic, [Fe/H], [α/Fe], [C/Fe], [N/Fe], and [Mg/Fe]—for eight million unique stars (11 million spectra including repeat observations) across the full parameter space. This adds a valuable dataset for studies of Galactic structure and archaeology, stellar populations, and related areas of astrophysics.
